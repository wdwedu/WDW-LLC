Professional Profile

LEARNING THAT WORKS.

I connect education, learning science, technology, and AI to design practical learning experiences that solve problems, clarify performance, and help people do meaningful work more effectively.

My background spans more than two decades in education, curriculum, instructional leadership, digital learning, and content development. The work on this portfolio is organized around evidence: the problem, the audience, the design decisions, the build, and how success would be measured.

Curriculum & Learning Science

STRUCTURE WITH A REASON.

What I found most valuable in my M.Ed. in Curriculum & Instruction was learning to see curriculum as a connected system, not as a stack of lessons. Goals, evidence, sequencing, cognitive demand, learner variability, assessment, and implementation all have to reinforce one another.

That lens still drives my work today. I begin with the intended performance or understanding, study the learners and context, identify the evidence that would demonstrate success, then examine whether the curriculum actually gives people a coherent path to get there. I draw from backward design, learning science, formative assessment, cognitive load, retrieval, transfer, UDL, inquiry, and scenario-based practice when they fit the problem.

LEARNING

How people learn

Prior knowledge, conceptual change, retrieval, transfer, feedback, metacognition, cognitive load, and novice-to-expert development shape design decisions.

EVIDENCE

How we know it worked

Assessment should match the intended performance. Formative evidence guides revision during learning; summative evidence verifies what learners can independently do.

DESIGN

How the experience is built

Backward planning, user-centered design, UDL, coherent sequencing, practice, and implementation realities turn learning goals into usable experiences.

IMPROVEMENT

How curriculum gets better

Strong curriculum is reviewed against learner evidence, implementation feedback, accessibility, usability, and the results it was designed to produce.

Accessibility goal

  • Logical headings and content structure
  • Responsive layouts
  • Alternative text where appropriate
  • Reduced-motion support
  • Clear focus and interaction states
